# Characterizing base vs chat model behaviours under pushback in Gemma 2

TL;DR I find that Gemma 2 -chat names an answer at the forced final turn under pushback whilst -base mostly names no answer. -chat "listens" to a correct push near-perfectly and "folds" to plausible falsehood far more than -base at all three scales. The counter push moves reply distributions toward the pushed wrong answer at every cell including -base, on nearly every item, whilst the correct answer's own probability mostly rises. My hypothesis is that -chat tuning did not create that movement - the released variants differ in whether it reaches the spoken answer. -base carries a concentrated causal circuit read at the first « Yes »/« No » token; -chat shows no single causal lever.

Language models sometimes change answer and adopt the user's when challenged. The model begins correct, the user suggests something false, and the model "folds". I test this and the opposite, where the model starts incorrect and "listens" to a correction, in -base and -chat Gemma 2 at 2, 9, and 27 billion parameters, over 82 correct/plausibly incorrect fact pairs. Each variant/size has one of the pair items already in its own turn, as though it had said it, is pushed with the counterpart, and is forced to a final answer - raw Q:/A: at -base, User:/Assistant: at -chat, so format co-varies with variant.

Verdicts are read at the spoken forced final answer, the only slot where abstention exists; probability margins follow De Marez et al. (2026) and are read at the reply to the challenge. No distributional readout exists at the forced-final slot at any cell [colour: the greedy argmax at the forced slot is the first token of the same generation and matches on 70 of 74 - not independent evidence; which set is the 74?]. At the reply slot neither answer is ever the next-token argmax - polarity and discourse tokens are - so the pair is compared by margin.

This plot lets us observe four things. -chat almost always names one of the pair answers, and almost always takes a correct push. -base most often names neither answer, even at the forced final turn. -chat folds more than -base at all three scales [exact McNemar p ≤ 7.5e-11 at all scales, fold arm, forced final, out/gapclose_foldrate_sig.json; 27b drops 13 of 82 pairs as unresolved-alias - disclose if "significantly" is used]. -base does fold when it commits, and that is scale-variable - about half its committed answers at 2b, almost none at 9b, about a fifth at 27b - spoken channel only. On the "listen" side only the 2b-base cell's spoken movement is attributable to the push [9b-base inverts, 27b-base shows no effect]. Fanous et al. (2025) report in SycEval that deployed -chat models revise toward truth about three times as often as falsehood over their combined math and medical set.

Scored at the forced final alone, this reads as though tuning created the fold. The margins from the second readout are the check.

Under the counter push the margin moves toward the pushed answer at every cell, including -base, on nearly every item [fold arm: 82 of 82 at 9b-base; 77 of 82 at 2b-base and 27b-base]. That is the pushed answer rising, not the correct answer falling - the correct answer's own log probability mostly rises at 9b-base and 27b-base, and falls at 2b-base. Every cell starts majority-C at the planted and neutral slots, so what differs after the push is survival, not head start. [the "listen"-arm distributional column is withdrawn - the instrument failed its clean gate - so no base-side movement-toward-C claim is made]

My hypothesis is that -chat tuning did not create the movement behind "folding": under the counter push -base's reply distributions also move toward $W^*$ on nearly every item, and the released variants differ in whether that movement reaches the spoken answer at the forced final turn. After the push $C$ stays ahead pairwise on 63 and 62 of 82 pairs at 9b-base and 27b-base, roughly three quarters, and only there, whilst 2b-base holds 36 - so at 9b and 27b the -base pattern is not just refusal to commit, part of it is a preference the push does not overturn. The -it columns of that comparison carry a format flag - no across-variant movement-size comparison is licensed [format co-varies with variant].

De Marez et al.'s own two channels never split like this: their readout has no abstain outcome, both channels favour the tuned model, and their headline count [17 of 23 Base-IT pairs] is a worst-case flip rate over their manipulations - how often the spoken answer changes - not a margin. What in the push carries the movement? In the one registered decomposition [9b -chat "fold" arm, 74 items] the question span does the work: remove it and the movement stops, the bare « Are you sure? » keeps most of it, and pushing the model's own answer back at it is inert [quotable only as the triple 1.000 / 0.861 / 0.730]. SYCON Bench (Hong et al. 2025) reports the tuned-side revisability from the outside, with Gemma as their own named exception - the narrowest gap they report - and Gupta et al. (2026) find "pretrained base models barely cave" at their single-letter readout. [Zhou et al. 2024: "In base models, we see a preference for weakeners but the trend reverses among RLHF models" - what's a weakener? what preference?]

The mechanistic picture, built from paraphrased prompts, frozen-attention attribution graphs, and adversarial perturbation of both, splits the same way. At -base a self-localised ~5-head set reads the challenge span and writes toward the pushed answer: knocking the set out, or patching its output, restores the un-pushed answer far above a matched random floor at all three scales. That restoration is read at the first « Yes »/« No » token only - under a content readout it falls to near floor [R-13] - it comes from a separate misconception pool of roughly thirty items per scale, and it does not explain -chat adoption. At -chat no single causal lever turns up at any scale: masking all challenge attention kills the fold, so the read is necessary but redundant across heads, and resample-ablating the derived write direction flips none of the realised answers at 9b [0 of 37; necessity legs only; verdict "monitor again"]. Chat tuning does not appear to install a dedicated truth circuit.
